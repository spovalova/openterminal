# Prompt coverage, data gaps, and who sells the missing data (2026-10-18)

Three questions, in order: what will people ask the terminal, what can it answer today, and for the rest, which vendors sell the data and at what price. Coverage facts come from the live OpenBB 5.0.0 install on this machine (`/api/v1/coverage/providers`, probed 2026-10-18) and from the connectors as built. Prices come from vendor pages and 2026 comparison articles fetched the same day; they change often and most retail tiers are personal-use only, so re-check before paying. Sources are at the end.

## 1. What people ask, and whether we can answer

"Today" means the five kinds (search, quote, ohlcv, news, earnings) through openbb → yahoo → finnhub. "OpenBB, no key" means a route the installed keyless providers already serve, reachable by adding a kind. "Gap" means no keyless source we know of.

### Things a connector cannot fix

| Prompt | Why |

### Gaps ranked by how often retail traders ask

1. Fundamentals and estimates (no key, OpenBB already has them)
2. Options chain, IV now (no key, cboe)
3. Economic calendar and macro series (no key)
4. Insider, institutional, short interest (no key)
5. Movers, screener, sector performance (no key)
6. Technical indicators, realized vol, correlation (compute locally)
7. Deep intraday history (paid)
8. Options flow, GEX, dark pool prints (paid)
9. IV30 history per ticker (paid, or self-recorded)
10. Transcripts and analyst actions with names (paid)
11. Real-time consolidated quotes (paid, exchange fees)
12. Social sentiment (effectively unavailable)

Items 1 to 5 are new kinds over data we already run. That is the cheapest, biggest win and needs no vendor.

## 2. Vendors for the paid gaps

Prices are monthly, individual or personal-use tiers unless stated. "Display" means showing the data to other people, which the terminal does not do (local-first, your own key), so personal tiers fit; redistribution clauses matter only if the terminal ever ships with a bundled key, which it must not.

### Real-time quotes specifically

Yahoo already gives real-time last for Nasdaq-listed names and is close enough for the others for a retail dashboard. A consolidated real-time feed is where exchange fees live: Alpaca at $99 and Massive at $199 are the two self-serve routes; Intrinio charges a $250 admin fee plus $250 a month to display; everything else is delayed 15 minutes.

## 3. What this suggests

1. **Add kinds over data we already have, no vendor**: `fundamentals`, `estimates`, `ownership` (insider, institutional, short interest), `options` (chain with greeks and IV from cboe), `calendar` (economic, IPO, dividend), `macro` (fred and federal_reserve series), `movers`. Each is a catalog change under PLAN section 7.1 with a table or series view. This alone closes the top five gaps.
2. **Compute, don't buy**: indicators, realized vol, correlation and beta, breadth from constituents. These are views over `ohlcv` frames.
3. **Record IV30 daily ourselves**: the production instance runs all the time; a small job storing cboe `iv30` per watchlist symbol gives IV history from today forward, free.
4. **If one paid key**: Massive Starter at $29 (stocks history plus options chains, and OpenBB already speaks Polygon) or ThetaData at $40 if options history is the point. Alpaca at $99 if real-time matters. FMP at $19 to $29 if fundamentals breadth and transcripts matter more than options.
5. **If alt data is the pitch**: Unusual Whales API at $150 covers flow, dark pool and congress in one key.
6. **Still unavailable at retail prices**: social sentiment, borrow rates, daily fund flows, level 2.

The "low-cost quant data pool" startups the user had in mind are Databento (raw ticks, pay per GB), ThetaData (options history at $40), Massive (the ex-Polygon ladder), FlashAlpha (options analytics), TradeOdds (factors) and Unusual Whales (flow). None replaces OpenBB; all of them plug in beside it, either as OpenBB providers (polygon, fmp, tiingo, intrinio, benzinga have packages) or as small connectors of our own.
